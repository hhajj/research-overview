# Research Overview

General directions in AI and cybersecurity research. This repository is intentionally high-level — unpublished artifacts, datasets, screening matrices, and operational data are kept private.

## Directions

### Detection under deployment drift

Machine-learning intrusion detection is usually evaluated on fixed datasets or synthetic drift. I am interested in what happens after deployment: how detection quality degrades as networks and threats change, how that degradation is measured, and how adaptation can be evaluated with temporal validity.

### Agentic AI for cyber defence

Defensive agents act on telemetry, tools, and evidence. My interest is in the integrity of that chain: whether the evidence an agent receives is trustworthy, whether actions are verified before they take effect, where capability boundaries should sit, and how decisions can be audited and attributed.

### Adversarial ML and security of AI-enabled defence

AI-assisted defence introduces its own attack surface. I study manipulation of model inputs and evidence, the robustness of AI-assisted security systems, and the failure modes that appear when automated defence is deployed against an adaptive adversary.

### Deception and active defence

Honeypots, adaptive deception, and moving-target defence raise the cost and uncertainty of attack. I am interested in how deception can be designed and measured in real operational environments, and how AI changes both the design and the adversary's ability to detect it.

## Approach

- Start from operational reality: real telemetry, real constraints, real failure modes.
- Treat AI output as a proposal, not ground truth; verify before acting.
- Prefer reproducible, temporally valid evaluation over benchmark-only claims.
- Keep unpublished material and sensitive operational data private.

## Status

Research activity is at a pre-publication stage: systematic literature reviews and research-oriented systems developed in 2026. No peer-reviewed publications to date.
